1. Learn the big ideas

Short explanations first. These ideas come directly from the Unit 2 text set.

Big Idea 1

“We the People” means citizens have power

The Constitution begins with “We the People.” The study guide explains that government power belongs to the citizens.

  • The United States is a representative democracy, also called a republic.
  • Citizens elect representatives to make government decisions.
  • Citizens can choose different leaders in future elections.
Big Idea 2

Federal laws and state laws are different

Federal means national. Federal laws affect people across the whole country. State laws focus on people living in that state.

Memory clue: Federal = whole country. State = one state.

Federal examples from the text

  • Women over 18 have the right to vote.
  • Citizens can worship any religion they choose.

Georgia examples from the text

  • Students must go to school 180 days each year.
  • Drivers cannot hold a phone in their hands while driving.

The three branches

Both the federal government and Georgia have legislative, executive, and judicial branches.

L

Legislative

Main job: makes and passes laws and approves the budget.

Federal leader: Congress

Georgia leader: The General Assembly

E

Executive

Main job: enforces laws.

Federal leader: the president

Georgia leader: the governor

J

Judicial

Main job: reviews laws to decide whether they are fair or unfair.

Federal leader: the Supreme Court

Georgia leader: the Supreme Court of Georgia

L = Laws
E = Enforces
J = Judges review laws

Federal government details

  • Congress makes and passes national laws.
  • Congress approves the national budget.
  • The president leads the executive branch.
  • The president is Commander in Chief of the U.S. military.
  • The Supreme Court leads the judicial branch.
  • The Supreme Court has nine justices.

Georgia government details

  • The General Assembly makes and passes state laws.
  • The General Assembly approves the state budget.
  • The governor leads the executive branch.
  • The governor lives and works in Atlanta, the state capital.
  • The Supreme Court of Georgia leads the judicial branch.
  • The Supreme Court of Georgia has nine justices.

Why three branches?

The Constitution separates government into three branches so one person or group cannot control everything. Each branch has its own powers and jobs so one branch does not have more power than the others.

Rights, responsibilities and the common good

Rights

Things the Constitution protects

  • Speak about a topic important to you.
  • Choose any religion.
  • Vote when you are 18 years old.
Responsibilities

Things citizens should do

  • Respect other citizens' rights.
  • Treat others with respect.
  • Obey rules and laws.
Common Good

Helping everyone in the community

  • Stay informed.
  • Vote when old enough.
  • Volunteer.
  • Communicate with public officials.

Examples of promoting the common good

Follow rules so everyone stays safe, do not litter, learn about community news, help others, and send a letter or email to a public official when you have a concern or something positive to share.
